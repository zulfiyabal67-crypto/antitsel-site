The Antigoal · A gentle practice

What do I no longer want?

Make room for yourself by noticing what you no longer want to keep as it is.

You do not need to know your next big goal. You can begin with one honest answer.

This short guide invites you to pause, listen to yourself, and choose one realistic next step. Move at your own pace.

Zulfiya Islamova · Creator of the Antigoal method

01 · Pause

You do not have to solve everything today.

When life feels difficult, we often try to find the right goal or make a complete plan. Sometimes a gentler starting point is to notice what has become too much.

What am I tired of carrying, repeating, or accepting as if nothing could change?

Do not judge your answer or rush to fix it. Write down what comes to mind, even if it feels unfinished.

Notice · No need to find a perfect answer

02 · Name what no longer fits

What do I no longer want?

Try to describe a situation or pattern, rather than criticising yourself or another person.

Always saying yesPutting rest offDoing everything aloneIgnoring my needsWaiting for “someday”
I no longer want to…

Use your own words. One clear sentence is enough.

Notice · Be specific and kind to yourself

03 · Look more closely

What is happening in this situation?

Choose one situation that matters to you right now. Separate what you can observe from what you assume it means.

The situationWhat actually happens? Describe it as simply as you can.
My responseWhat do I usually do, agree to, avoid, or postpone?
The cost to meWhat does this pattern take from my time, energy, attention, or peace?
Clarify · Curiosity instead of blame

04 · Find your point of influence

What can I change?

Not everything is under your control. You do not have to change other people or solve the whole situation to find one possible point of influence.

I can stop or reduce…One unnecessary task, automatic yes, or repeated commitment.
I can ask for…Help, time to think, clearer expectations, or a conversation.
I can do differently…Pause before answering, protect a little time, or explain a need.
One part of this situation I may be able to influence is…
Clarify · Start with what is within reach

05 · Choose a small step

What could I do differently?

Choose one action that fits your current energy and circumstances. It should be specific enough that you can tell when you have done it.

Instead of: “I need to stop being overwhelmed.”
Try: “Tomorrow, I will ask for help with one task.”
Instead of: “I need to put myself first.”
Try: “Before agreeing to the next request, I will pause and check my capacity.”
My next small step is…
Take a step · Small is allowed

06 · Make it realistic

Can I really do this now?

A useful step does not have to be impressive. It needs to be manageable in your real life—not in an ideal week when everyone leaves you alone and every problem is solved.

When?Choose a day or a moment.
What might get in the way?Notice one likely obstacle without blaming yourself.
How can I make it smaller?Reduce the action until it feels possible.
I will take this step…

My step feels manageable with the energy and resources I have right now.

Take a step · Adjust, do not punish yourself

07 · Return to yourself

You are allowed to move at your own pace.

You may not be able to change everything. You may not feel certain about what comes next. That does not make this pause pointless.

The Antigoal practice is not about giving up on dreams or rejecting every goal. It is a way to notice what you no longer want to continue unchanged, and to make space for a more conscious choice.

What do I want to remember about myself after this practice?
One clear observation. One possible change. One next step. That is enough for now.
