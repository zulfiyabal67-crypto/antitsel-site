Page 2 · Begin here

Where Do I Begin?

When it is difficult to answer the question, “What do I want?”

There are times when we realise that something in our lives has not felt right for a long time. Yet it can be hard to say what we want instead.

In the Anti-Goal Method, we begin with a different question:

What do I no longer want?

Look at your life today: your relationships, work, responsibilities, communication, rest, and the way you treat yourself.

Complete the sentence a few times:

I no longer want…

Read your answers again. Which one stands out most strongly? Underline it. That is the one we will explore next.

Page 3 · Notice the pattern

How Long Have I Been Living with This?

We gradually get used to certain things—even when they have not suited us for a long time.

Take the sentence you chose on the previous page and reflect on it:

1 · How long has this been going on?
2 · When did I first feel that this was not right for me?
3 · What do I usually tell myself when I put off making a change?
4 · One more questionIf I have known for a long time that this does not suit me, why has nothing changed so far?

You do not need to judge yourself for your answers. For now, simply notice what is happening.

Page 4 · Put it into words

My Anti-Goal

Now try to name more precisely what you no longer want to put up with.

1 · What exactly feels unacceptable to me in this situation?
2 · What do I keep doing, tolerating, or supporting?
3 · What am I doing here that goes against my own needs?

Now formulate your Anti-Goal in your own words, focusing on yourself and your actions.

For example:
“I no longer want to stay silent when someone speaks to me in a hurtful way.”
“I no longer want to agree to extra work when I am already exhausted, simply because I am afraid to refuse.”

Page 5 · Understand what holds you back

What Is Keeping Me Here?

If the situation continues, there may be reasons it feels difficult to change. Look at them gently and honestly.

1 · What am I afraid might happen if I begin to change something?
2 · What might I lose?
3 · Whose disappointment am I afraid of?
4 · What does keeping everything as it is allow me to avoid?

It might be a familiar routine, financial stability, a sense of security, or the possibility of avoiding conflict or a difficult decision.

Page 6 · See the cost of staying the same

The Cost of Keeping Things As They Are

Choosing not to change anything also has consequences.

1 · What am I already losing while everything stays the same?
2 · Imagine that another year has passed and the situation has not changed. What might my life look like?
3 · How do I feel when I imagine this?
4 · Am I willing to keep living this way?

The cost may involve time, energy, opportunities, interest in life, relationships, or respect for yourself.

Page 7 · Find your next step

What Is Within My Control?

Look at the situation once more. Separate what you cannot control from what you can influence.

1 · What is not within my control?
2 · What does depend on me?

Now choose one action that belongs in the second group.

3 · What can I do in the next seven days?

The first step can be small: having a conversation, saying no, asking for help, looking for information, or making a decision you have been postponing.

Page 8 · A reminder for the road ahead

You Can Do This

This guide is not a magic solution. It is a support for your own path.

Remember:

♡ You are already taking an important step by asking yourself honest questions.
❧ You have the right to change what no longer feels right for you.
✳ Small changes matter, too.
☆ You do not have to figure everything out at once.

“Sometimes the biggest change begins with a small, honest question.”

Return to this guide whenever you feel tangled or unsure. These questions are here to help you hear yourself more clearly.
