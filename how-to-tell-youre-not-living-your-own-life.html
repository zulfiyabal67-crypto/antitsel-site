How to Tell When You’re No Longer Living Your Own Life

Why can a life that looks perfectly fine from the outside still feel as though there is too little room for you? Here is how to notice the signs and where to begin.

Sometimes, everything looks perfectly normal from the outside. Work, family, everyday tasks, plans. You manage things, make decisions, and take care of the people close to you.

Yet every now and then, a strange feeling appears:

“I seem to be living my own life, so why does it feel as though there is so little of me in it?”

For many women, this question becomes more familiar after 40. Not because reaching a certain age inevitably brings a crisis. By then, many women have spent decades building a life around all kinds of things they feel they “have to” do.

You have to be a good daughter. You have to get an education. You have to work. You have to care for your family. You have to be strong. You must not let anyone down.

And one day, the familiar rules stop working.

You do a lot, but you increasingly wonder why

One of the first signs is that life starts to feel like a long to-do list.

Wake up. Do things. Reply. Help. Decide. Keep up.

You may be a very active, capable woman. The people around you might not even notice that anything has changed.

But inside, a question comes up more and more often:

“Do I still want all of this for myself?”

And you may not have an answer.

This does not necessarily mean you need to quit your job, end your relationship, or start your life over. Sometimes the problem is not your life itself, but the fact that you have not checked in with your own wishes for a long time.

You think more about what is right than about what you want

Try paying attention to how you make decisions.

Which thoughts do you hear most often?

“This is the right thing to do.” “What will people say?” “I can’t just walk away.” “I have to.” “This is what everyone expects of me.”

Of course, we cannot live by our wishes alone. We all have responsibilities and commitments.

The problem begins when your own voice no longer has a place in your decisions at all.

Your life is good, but somehow it brings you little joy

Sometimes, a woman’s life is objectively going well. That can make it especially difficult to admit to herself that she is unhappy, or that she has not felt much interest in her own life for a long time.

Guilt follows: “I have everything. What more could I possibly need?”

But being grateful for what you have and wanting to change something are not mutually exclusive.

You can value your family and still want more space for yourself. You can love your work and recognise that it no longer gives you what it once did.

You can be grateful for the life you have lived and still want to live the next part of it a little differently.

How can you tell where “I have to” ends and you begin?

You do not have to start by asking yourself, “What do I want from life?”

For someone who has spent a long time guided by duties and other people’s expectations, that question can feel too big.

That is why, in my Antigoal method, I suggest starting with a different question:

“What do I no longer want?”

I no longer want to rush all the time. I no longer want to say yes out of guilt. I no longer want to be strong every single moment. I no longer want to do work I have outgrown. I no longer want to put myself off for another few years.

Sometimes it is easier to recognise what has become too constraining in your old life than to define a brand-new goal.

You do not have to change your whole life

When you realise how much you are living by old rules, it can be tempting to think that only a radical decision will solve the problem.

But big changes do not always begin with big decisions.

Sometimes it is enough to say no to something you used to agree to automatically. Take back a few hours each week. Try something you have wanted to do for a long time. Stop explaining every decision you make to everyone around you.

Ask yourself:

“If I keep living exactly this way for another five years, will that be okay with me?”

Your answer may help you see things more clearly.

Because living your own life does not mean living a perfect life in which you do only what you want.

It means that, among all your duties, roles, and other people’s expectations, there is still room for you.
