Why Do I Keep Putting My Life on Hold?

Sometimes we wait so long for the right moment that life itself slowly turns into waiting.

Sometimes a woman says, “Let me get a few things sorted out first—and then I’ll focus on myself.”

Work will become less difficult. The children will become more independent. Family issues will be resolved. There will be more money. Life will become calmer.

Then, at last, she will be able to rest, travel somewhere, return to something she used to enjoy, and think about herself.

There is just one problem: that “then” keeps moving further away.

A life that is supposed to begin later

Putting life on hold rarely looks like doing nothing.

On the contrary, a woman may be very active. She works, takes care of loved ones, solves dozens of problems and makes plans. From the outside, her life seems full.

But gradually, a strange feeling appears inside: I am always doing something, yet it feels as though I am not really living.

Because the present becomes preparation for the future.

“I just need to get through this for now. This is not the right time. Once this period is over, something else will begin.”

A year passes. Then another.

Circumstances change, but the habit of waiting remains.

Why is it so easy to agree to “later”?

I do not think it is always about a lack of time.

Sometimes “later” simply feels safer than “now”.

As long as I only dream about change, I do not have to change anything. I do not have to make difficult decisions, face someone’s disappointment, risk the familiar way of life or ask myself a question I may not yet be ready to answer:

“How do I actually want to live?”

This can be especially hard to answer if, for many years, the main guide has been not our own wishes, but what our family, work, children or parents need.

Gradually, a life of our own starts to seem like something we can have only when special circumstances fall into place.

Sometimes we are waiting for permission

There is something else that can be hard to notice.

Many women are very good at fulfilling their duties, but find it much harder to allow themselves something that has no obvious practical benefit.

Simply resting. Buying something for themselves. Spending a day as they wish. Turning down an invitation. Learning something that has interested them for a long time. Travelling somewhere they have wanted to visit for years.

There is always a reason to postpone it.

It is too expensive right now. It is inconvenient. There are more important things to do. Something else must be finished first.

And all of these reasons may be perfectly reasonable.

But if they appear year after year, it may be worth asking yourself:

Am I really waiting for the right moment, or have I grown used to not choosing myself?

A life is made up of many small “not now” decisions

We rarely decide to put our entire lives on hold all at once.

Usually, it happens much more quietly.

Today, you do not go for a walk, even though you really wanted to. This year, once again, you do not travel to the place you have been planning to visit. You do not return to a favourite activity. You do not call the person you wanted to hear from. You do not begin something you have been thinking about for years.

Each episode seems insignificant on its own.

But years are gradually built from these small decisions.

And one day, what hurts most is not how much you have done, but how many times your own life was left for later.

You do not have to start a new life

When we notice this pattern, it is easy to swing to the other extreme and decide that everything must change immediately.

But big changes are not always necessary.

In the Antigoal method, another question matters more to me:

What in my life do I no longer want to keep doing the way I always have?

Not “What should my new life look like?”

Not “What must I achieve?”

But what do I no longer want to keep postponing?

Sometimes the answer is quite small.

I no longer want to postpone rest until I am completely exhausted.

I do not want to cancel my plans every time someone needs something.

I do not want to spend another year saying that one day I will return to what I love.

Then change no longer looks like a huge leap into the unknown.

There is one small step that can be taken now.

In closing

The perfect moment—when all problems are solved, no one needs anything from us, there is enough money and nothing but free time lies ahead—probably will not come.

Life rarely clears a special space for us.

Perhaps that is why it matters to create that space ourselves, little by little.

Not someday.

In the life that is already happening.
