Why Is It So Hard to Ask for Help?

Sometimes the hardest words to say are not the ones we speak out loud.

There are women people always turn to.

If someone needs help looking after a child, they call them. If someone gets sick, they are the first to offer support. If a friend is struggling, they are ready to listen, even late in the evening. They almost always find the time, energy, and right words for others.

But one day, they are the ones who need help.

And suddenly, everything becomes much more complicated.

“No, thank you. I can do it myself.”

“Would you like some help?”

“No, thank you. I can do it myself.”

This conversation happens again and again. Even when things are getting difficult, energy is running out, and a little help would make all the difference.

Over time, “I can do it myself” becomes a reflex. We say it almost automatically, without asking whether we really need to manage alone this time.

When accepting help feels unfamiliar

After 40, many women notice that their tiredness feels different.

They wish someone would give them a lift, collect some documents, make dinner, or simply say, “Take the evening off. I’ll take care of everything.”

But instead, they hear their own voice again:

“No, it’s fine. I’ll do it myself.”

And suddenly they realise they cannot remember the last time they calmly accepted someone else’s help.

The habit of coping on your own

Year after year, being the person everyone can rely on becomes familiar. Gradually, you stop noticing that you have not asked for help in a long time. It seems easier this way. More peaceful. You do not have to trouble anyone.

Then one day, you realise there are people around you who are willing to help. But you have almost forgotten how to let them.

Where the conversation begins

When we first meet, the conversation rarely begins with the words:

“I need help.”

More often, women talk about work, family, responsibilities, and tiredness.

Then, almost in passing, they say:

“I think I can’t manage everything on my own anymore.”

After those words, something in the conversation usually changes.

We stop talking about how to fit everything in and begin looking for an answer to a different question.

Why have I had to manage everything on my own for so many years?
