Why Have I Stopped Feeling Joy?

Sometimes joy has not disappeared. There is simply no room left for it.

There comes a moment when a woman suddenly notices that life seems to have lost its colour.

Nothing terrible has happened. The home is still there, she has a job, and her loved ones are nearby. Yet the things that used to bring joy no longer stir anything inside. Favourite books remain unopened. Meeting friends no longer feels exciting. Even a long-awaited holiday does not bring a sense of rest.

And then an unsettling thought appears:

“What is wrong with me?”

Very often, the problem is not you.

Joy does not disappear in a single day

It fades gradually.

First, you get used to putting your wishes aside for other people’s needs. Then you begin to live by a list of obligations. After that, you stop noticing your own feelings because there is simply no time left for them.

And one day, you realise that everything inside has gone quiet.

Not peaceful. Empty.

We often mistake exhaustion for losing interest in life

When a person lives under pressure for a long time, the mind begins to conserve energy.

It dampens not only anxiety and pain. Pleasure, inspiration and curiosity can grow quieter, too. This is not weakness or laziness. It can be a way of protecting inner resources when very little is left.

So a lack of joy does not always mean you have forgotten how to feel it. Sometimes it means you have been living in a state of constant strain for too long.

You cannot force joy to return

When people find themselves in this state, many start looking for ways to enjoy life again.

They buy new things, plan trips, sign up for courses and try to think positively. These things may lift the mood for a while, but they rarely change the inner state on their own.

Joy is not a reward for completing a to-do list correctly. It is a natural feeling that can emerge when a person no longer has to keep fighting herself.

Sometimes, instead of searching for joy, we need to remove what is getting in its way

Often, joy is crowded out not by external events, but by familiar inner rules. They quietly shape our lives: duty first, rest later; everyone else first, me later; earn the right to live, then allow yourself to do so. Over time, these rules can seem like the only possible way to live, even though they may be a source of constant inner tension.

As long as these rules remain unnoticed, joy moves further into the background. Not because it is gone, but because there is no inner space left for it.

What can you do?

The first step is not to force yourself to feel happy.

It is more important to look honestly at your life and ask yourself a few simple questions.

When was the last time I did something not because I “had to”, but because I wanted to?

When did I last allow myself to rest without feeling guilty?

When did I ask myself what I wanted?

Sometimes these questions mark the beginning of meaningful inner change.

In closing

In the Antigoal method, we do not teach ourselves to feel joy on command.

We begin to notice what has quietly drained our energy and diminished our sense of being alive over the years. As inner tension eases, there may be more room for interest, lightness and our own wishes.

Then joy no longer has to be chased.

It can gradually return on its own.

Not because life has become perfect, but because there is room for you in it again.
