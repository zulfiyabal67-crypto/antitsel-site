Why Does Someone Else’s Success Drain Your Energy?

Why we compare ourselves with others, feel left behind, and how to reclaim the right to move at our own pace

You open your social media feed. Or you run into someone you used to know. She has a business, travels, a seemingly perfect family, and new achievements.

You look at your own life and feel something tighten inside.

It is not even envy in the usual sense. It is a heavy, sticky feeling that you are “falling behind”—that something is wrong with you.

And the most frustrating part is that you know this comparison is unfair, but you cannot seem to stop.

Why does this happen?

Because we often compare our inner experience with someone else’s outer appearance.

You see another person’s results and the polished picture they share. You do not see their sleepless nights, doubts, exhaustion, or personal struggles.

You compare your behind-the-scenes reality with their public-facing image. It is an unfair contest from the start—one you cannot win.

After 40, this feeling can become more intense. For much of our lives, we may have believed there was a “right” timeline: a career by 30, stability by 40, wisdom and peace by 50.

If your life does not fit that template, it can feel as though you have failed.

But whose timeline is it? And why must you live by it?

“Everyone is doing better than I am”

This feeling is especially familiar to many women over 40.

Friends post holiday photos. Colleagues get promoted. People you know buy homes or start their own businesses.

And you?

You have an ordinary life, with its routines, tiredness, and the feeling that you are standing still.

But here is what matters: you cannot see their private conversations with themselves, their fears, or the times when they also feel they are not coping.

Every woman goes through periods when it seems that everyone else has already arrived “there,” while she is still “here.”

That does not mean something is wrong with you.

It means you are living your life.

When someone else’s success feels like a threat

Sometimes another person’s achievements feel less like inspiration and more like a threat.

As if her success automatically means your failure.

As if life were a zero-sum game in which one person’s win must be someone else’s loss.

But life does not work that way.

Another woman’s success does not take anything away from you.

Her path is hers. Your path is yours.

They do not have to compete.

What may really be behind the comparison

Often, comparing yourself with someone else is not really about wanting exactly what they have.

It may point to a need of your own that you have not yet put into words.

The next time you catch yourself thinking, “I wish my life were like that,” pause for a moment. Do not scold yourself.

Simply ask: “What exactly about her life has affected me?”

Perhaps it is not her new car. Perhaps it is her sense of calm, her ability to say no, or the fact that she has finally started doing something she loves.

This reaction is not a reason to copy someone else’s life.

It may be a clue about what you need in your own.

How can you stop comparing yourself?

You may not be able to eliminate comparison entirely; comparing ourselves is a common human tendency.

But you can learn to notice it and gently bring your attention back to yourself.

Acknowledge the comparison. Do not criticise yourself for it. Simply notice: “I’m comparing myself again.”

Ask yourself: “What exactly am I feeling? What do I need right now?”

Remind yourself: “Her path is not my path. I am moving at my own pace.”

Return to yourself. What matters to you? What can you do for yourself today?

In the Antigoal method, we do not begin with grand ambitions. We begin with an honest question: “What do I no longer want?”

Perhaps you no longer want to live by someone else’s clock. Perhaps you no longer want to feel guilty because your path looks different.

Your life is not late. It is simply following its own route.
